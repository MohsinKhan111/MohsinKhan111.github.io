Research

Research and experimentation across AI and emerging technology, from local language models to game development and hardware.

  1. Ongoing 2025

    Offline LLMs

    Building a permanent offline environment for running and keeping language models without internet access, APIs, subscriptions, or cloud services.

    I use open weight models with llama.cpp, keep them on dedicated local storage, and maintain offline backups so the environment can be moved to other machines.

    I test these models on limited hardware and document their practical capabilities and limits.

  2. Ongoing 2025

    Small Language Models

    Experimenting with building small language models from scratch to understand how they work.

    The work covers datasets, tokenization, model architecture, training, inference, quantization, and evaluation.

    When local hardware is not sufficient, I use rented GPU infrastructure for training and benchmarking while keeping development, datasets, code, and results under my control.

  3. Ongoing 2026

    AI Game Worlds

    Exploring how language models can be used inside 2D game worlds.

    The work explores character behaviour and decision making, allowing characters to respond to their surroundings and other characters rather than relying entirely on fixed scripts.

  4. Ongoing 2026

    AI & Robotics

    Experimenting with language models and small hardware projects.

    The work explores how local models can interact with sensors, physical hardware, and simple autonomous behaviour, using local and rented compute where appropriate.

  5. Ongoing 2026

    AI, Learning & Consciousness

    We still cannot fully explain how an AI reaches its answers.

    We understand the basic process: a model learns from enormous amounts of data as its parameters are adjusted during training. When given something to answer, information passes through its neural network layers as it calculates the next token, then the next. We can observe and measure this process, but we cannot fully explain how the learned representations and computations inside those layers produce a specific answer, or exactly how the model learned the relationships it uses.

    That gap is what interests me. I’m researching how these models learn, reason, and arrive at their outputs, using small models and interpretability techniques to investigate what happens inside rather than only judging what they produce.

    I’ve also developed ways to measure and test parts of this process, and I’m continuing to refine them through ongoing experiments. Some tools can trace parts of a model’s internal computation and investigate which features may contribute to an answer. They are still limited, but they give us ways to investigate something we cannot yet fully explain.

    I’m working on this daily. The deeper I get into it, the more interesting it becomes. At the centre is a question I keep coming back to: could understanding how increasingly complex systems learn eventually tell us something about consciousness?
