Running LLMs locally

What small language models can do offline on modest hardware, in software and hardware projects.

The question

Most capable models need hardware I don’t have. This experiment asks how far small, openly available models can go on modest compute, running fully offline, and how well they hold context on small, precisely defined tasks.

How

It starts with a local library of the models available to me, and a back end for downloading, managing and running them offline. With it, I run controlled experiments: very small, precise tasks, repeated across models, to see how each one handles context.

From there the work has two parts, and both are experimental.

Software. The models that hold up are tailored to short scripts and small software projects. One of those is a 2D life-simulation RPG: fifteen to twenty AI characters share one world, each with its own agenda. Because they’re driven by AI rather than fixed scripts, every playthrough unfolds differently.

Hardware. The same local, offline models are tailored to hardware functions, for a few small robotics projects.

What it showed

Still in progress. There are no results to report yet.

What it led to

Nothing final yet. The aim is to build the models that prove reliable into the tailored repository in experiment 05.
